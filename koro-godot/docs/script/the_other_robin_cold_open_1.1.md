# SEQUENCE 1: THE CITY BELONGS TO SOMEONE ELSE

**FADE IN:**

### SHOT 01. BLACK SCREEN
**FRAMING:** Absolute black. **DURATION:** 4 seconds. **CAMERA:** Static.
Rain falls against metal. A low electrical hum pulses beneath the sound, like a city breathing through its teeth. Somewhere far away, a siren rises and falls without getting any closer. A small mechanical CLICK. Silence for half a beat.
**THE DIRECTOR (V.O.)** (calm, practised) Ten years ago, the weapons got small enough to wear.
On the word "wear," a tiny red light appears for a fraction of a second, then disappears. CUT ON THE ELECTRICAL HUM.

### SHOT 02. FALLING THROUGH THE CLOUDS
**FRAMING:** Extreme wide aerial. **LENS:** 18mm. **CAMERA:** Slow descent through cloud, tilting toward the horizon.
The underside of a cloud fills the frame. The camera descends through it; building lights emerge below; Manhattan stretches across the horizon. The movement is almost imperceptible at first; as the skyline appears we realize we have been falling the entire time. Rain passes close to the lens. Drones drift between buildings at different depths; one crosses close, a red blur; another so distant it resembles a star.
**THE DIRECTOR (V.O.)** A ring. A bracelet. A pair of shoes.
Glimpses, not scenes: on "a ring," a commuter on a rooftop terrace turns their hand, a thin ring catching red; on "a bracelet," a courier on an electric motorcycle, a luminous band beneath their sleeve; on "a pair of shoes," a distant figure leaps between two rooftops. On "shoes," the camera pitches down toward the city.

### SHOT 03. THE TRAIN
**FRAMING:** Extreme wide lateral. **LENS:** 35mm. **CAMERA:** Tracking alongside an elevated train.
The train enters screen left; its windows glow white against the rain-black exterior; passengers in rows beneath cold light, looking at phones; nobody speaks. Every window displays WFC. A support pillar occludes the frame: a natural wipe.
**THE DIRECTOR (V.O.)** And the gangs wore them first.

### SHOT 04. NEON AVENUE
**FRAMING:** Street-level wide, inches above the road. **LENS:** 24mm. **CAMERA:** Slow forward dolly.
We emerge from behind the pillar at street level. Puddles reflect neon upside down. A car passes in the extreme foreground, sending water toward the lens. We pass a discarded toy, a broken drone rotor, a crumpled WFC public safety flyer caught against a storm drain. A flickering sign. Three teenagers beneath a broken storefront sign. One of them is JUNO, 17.

### SHOT 05. JUNO AND THE BRACELET
**FRAMING:** MCU, slightly below eye level. **LENS:** 50mm. **CAMERA:** Slow push-in.
Juno leans against the shutter of a closed shop; his friends at the edges, out of focus (one watches the road, the other laughs at a phone). He rolls his wrist; a bracelet wakes: a faint white pulse becoming saturated red-orange, lighting his fingers, his chin, his eyes. He sees his reflection in the shop window and grins at himself. He raises his arm. RACK FOCUS from his reflection to the bracelet. Full brightness. WHUMP.

### SHOT 06. THE WINDOW EXPLODES
**FRAMING:** Wide profile from across the street. **LENS:** 40mm. **CAMERA:** High-speed slow motion.
A fraction of a second of stillness; then the window EXPLODES outward: thousands of shards catching the neon, some rotating slowly, others shooting toward the lens. The low impact first, the shattering a beat later. Juno laughs. The camera tracks a single piece of glass tumbling through the rain; in its reflection, a little girl at a bus stop.

### SHOT 07. THE GIRL
**FRAMING:** Medium, through the glass of a bus shelter. **LENS:** 85mm. **CAMERA:** Nearly static.
The GIRL (9) covers her head; her mother pulls her down behind the bench, shields her head; glass strikes the shelter. In the shelter's glass: Juno, the storefront, the girl's face — three layers of the same street. Her eyes go to Juno.
**THE DIRECTOR (V.O.)** Our streets became battlegrounds. Our children grew up afraid.
On "afraid," a red light washes across her face: not the explosion, but a WFC vehicle arriving behind her.

### SHOT 08. THE WFC ARRIVES
**FRAMING:** Symmetrical extreme wide down the avenue. **LENS:** 32mm. **CAMERA:** Locked, then a slow push on Juno.
Two armored WFC vans from opposite ends; headlights meet in the centre; they brake, sliding sideways to block both ends. Officers in a line: grey armor, mirrored visors, unnerving coordination. Juno's friends run; Juno stays. The camera does not follow the runners; it pushes toward Juno, isolating him.

### SHOT 09. THE DRONE
**FRAMING:** Low-angle MCU. **LENS:** 65mm. **CAMERA:** Rapid tilt, then a slow push.
Juno raises his bracelet again; a drone drops into frame from above, hovering between him and the lens. Its red eye opens; a mechanical iris; three scanning lights across his face.
**DRONE** Unregistered wearable detected.
For the first time Juno looks afraid. Push past the drone: officers close from both sides; one grips his wrist; the bracelet goes dark. A tiny CLICK, like the click in the black.
**THE DIRECTOR (V.O.)** The World Field Command is here to take this city back.

### SHOT 10. THE ARREST
**FRAMING:** Low detail, tracking along the wet pavement. **LENS:** 50mm. **CAMERA:** Slow lateral track.
Never a conventional arrest shot: armored boots, a fallen phone, Juno's hand forced behind his back; the bracelet dark on the road; a boot nearly steps on it. Indistinct protest above frame.
**THE DIRECTOR (V.O.)** To make New York safe again.
The camera continues forward, then begins to rise.

### SHOT 11. THE BILLBOARD
**FRAMING:** Low-angle vertical reveal. **LENS:** 24mm. **CAMERA:** Continuous rising crane.
Past the storefronts, a row of windows, up a tower; a massive billboard enters from the top: a WOMAN in her fifties, immaculate WFC uniform, composed, almost maternal. Rising until her face dominates. WFC. PROTECTING NEW YORK. A thin border pulsing cool white. Rain runs down the billboard; a stream crosses one eye; she does not blink. Pull back: the billboard high above the street, drones across the skyline, the city beneath her swallowed by shadow.
SUPER: NEW YORK CITY · 2044. Hold one beat. SMASH CUT TO:

# SEQUENCE 2: ROBIN'S WORLD

### SHOT 12. THE GUITAR RIFF
ECU of a vibrating red guitar string (85mm), the pick scraping it; a distorted riff tears through the silence. A whip pan past the amp's glowing controls, cables, a sock hanging off the desk — landing on ROBIN RYU, 16, sprinting into frame.

### SHOT 13. ROBIN LANDS ON THE BED
Wide master from the foot of the bed (21mm), low handheld, reacting slightly behind her. She launches onto the bed, bounces once, twice, throws her arms up; the camera tilts slightly too late, then corrects. She sings, loudly and badly; she knows every word. Warm bedside lamp, the blue of a monitor, a red LED strip; the city's light moving on the walls. The room feels too small to contain her.

### SHOT 14. THE ROOM AS A MUSIC VIDEO
**14A** The sewing machine: low side-angle close-up; the needle through black technical fabric; her hand adjusts a seam; a spark from the soldering iron; rack to it, whip away.
**14B** The jacket: low-angle close-up on the dress form; illuminated lines down its sleeves; Robin bouncing on the bed in the deep background; a blue pulse.
**14C** The business: overhead insert; boxes, handwritten labels (one scribbled over and rewritten), a burner phone, cash under a mug covered in faded cartoon stickers, a half-finished circuit board.
**14D** The shoes: floor-level macro; white sneakers by the window, coils in the soles; a passing car's light makes the coils look electric; her socked feet slide into the foreground.

### SHOT 15. AIR-DRUMMING
Medium profile (35mm), lateral handheld. She slides in her socks, spins, grabs two screwdrivers and air-drums; one passes inches from the lens. Reverse angle: drumming toward camera. She hits the bench too hard; a tiny component jumps; she catches it without looking. She keeps singing.

### SHOT 16. THE GUITAR
Medium with foreground obstruction (50mm), slow push. She stops. A red guitar against the amp, half-hidden behind a hanging jacket. The camera slows with the song still going; a red reflection along its body; she grins and walks to it with exaggerated seriousness; strap over her shoulder. LOW ANGLE as she kicks the amp on. FEEDBACK SCREAMS.

### SHOT 17. GAMEPLAY: THE GUITAR
Opening riff: ECU of her picking hand, strings in the foreground, her face blurred behind. First verse: low angle from the floor, her towering, one sneaker on the bed. First chorus: fast orbit, the window, bench and dress form sweeping past. Guitar fill: over-the-shoulder close-up, fingers on the fretboard, city lights beyond. Second chorus: wide from atop the wardrobe; she jumps off the bed and the camera tilts down after her. Final riff: tight frontal close-up, hair whipping, lost in it. Final chord: low-angle hero; she jumps off the bed and lands on the chord; the camera follows, overshoots, settles.
She falls back onto the bed, guitar on her chest, laughing at nobody. The song ends. Silence; the amp's hum. Hold; let her breathing settle. Her phone vibrates against the mattress.

### SHOT 18. CANON CALLS
Overhead close-up of the phone on the bedspread (50mm, static). Her hand enters; she answers without looking.
**ROBIN** Mm. **CANON (V.O.)** You alive? You missed chem. Again.
Loose MCU, sitting on the bed, the guitar across her lap. **ROBIN** I was there. Spiritually.
She gets up and paces; a loose handheld track, waist up, past the bench, the boxes, the form, slower now.
**CANON (V.O.)** Teacher says one more missed lab write-up and she's calling home.

### SHOT 19. THE CLOSED DOOR
Deep-focus composition from inside the bedroom (35mm), slow lateral track. She walks toward the end of the flat, phone to her ear; the camera stays, watching her go. Bedroom light on the floorboards; the hallway dim; a closed door at the end, no light beneath it. She passes without looking at it.
**ROBIN** She can try. (another step) Nobody's ever home.
The camera does not push in. Her voice has changed slightly: a sentence she has said before and does not want to discuss. A refrigerator compressor starts somewhere.
**CANON (V.O.)** You're coming tomorrow, though. I'm not building that robot by myself.
Profile at the far end; a strip of warm light on her cheek. **ROBIN** Yes, Canon. I'll be there. **CANON (V.O.)** On time? (she smiles) **ROBIN** Don't push it.
Her phone BUZZES. The smile fades.

### SHOT 20. THE SECOND CALL
ECU of the phone (macro): UNKNOWN NUMBER over Canon's name; its cold light on her thumb. Her eyes flick to the boxes; playful becomes attentive.
**ROBIN** Hold on. Let me call you back. I have to do something. **CANON (V.O.)** Robin, wait...
She switches calls. Close-up from behind her shoulder, her face in the dark screen.
**BUYER (V.O.)** Hey. You got my order? (she turns to the boxes) **ROBIN** Yeah. It's ready.

### SHOT 21. THE RINGS
Top-down insert (macro), static then a subtle push. She opens a small black box with one thumb: two silver rings, almost ordinary; a faint light travelling round the inside of each. She tilts the box; a tiny metallic sound. Push until the rings fill the frame; her eye reflected in one. Medium: she closes the box, sets it down. Alone in her bedroom; the guitar against the bed; the amp humming. 22:15.
**ROBIN** (to herself) Okay.
She turns off the lamp. CUT TO BLACK FOR A FRACTION OF A SECOND.

# SEQUENCE 3: BECOMING THE OTHER ROBIN

### SHOT 22. THE JACKET
MCU from behind the dress form (50mm), slow slider. Her hand from the dark; the jacket lifts off the form and swings toward the lens, black; it clears and she's putting it on. The lines in the sleeves activate one after another, wrists to shoulders — a system booting up. She rolls her shoulders; it settles.

### SHOT 23. THE GLOVES
Tight hand insert (macro). The gloves beside the soldering iron; one hand, then the other; palms together; a faint metallic snap. She grips the edge of the bench; holds; releases. Equipment being used, not explained.

### SHOT 24. THE SHOES
Floor-level close-up (35mm), tilt down into a low track. She kneels by the window and laces the sneakers; the coils flicker blue as the laces pull tight, light across the floorboards. She presses one heel down: a quiet mechanical compression; the coils go dark. She stands; the camera tilts just enough to follow her feet leaving the floor. No jump yet.

### SHOT 25. THE BANGLE AND BACKPACK
ECU: the bangle snaps on; an indicator lights and dims. Over the shoulder (35mm): the ring box into the backpack's front pocket, snug; roll-top closed; over one shoulder; strap adjusted in one practised movement; her hand from the strap to her collar. No mirror. No heroic pose. She is getting ready for work — not the kind most sixteen-year-olds have.

### SHOT 26. THE DINNER
Wide static through the kitchen doorway (40mm). She crosses toward the table; small, clean flat; a plate under a metal cover, a glass of water untouched; cooler light, quiet, unoccupied. INSERT: "Dinner's in the fridge. Love you. — Mom." Rain on the kitchen window. Close-up: she does not cry; her eyes pause at "Love"; hold one second longer than a normal reaction. She looks down the hallway; the closed door deep in the background.

### SHOT 27. THE DOOR
Long lens down the hallway (85mm), static. The door centred; Robin a soft silhouette in the foreground. Nothing happens. No music, no voice. She squares the note with the table edge, exactly as it was. She walks away. The camera stays on the note; for a moment it is the only thing in focus. CUT TO THE BEDROOM.

### SHOT 28. THE SUNGLASSES
Tight frontal close-up (75mm), slow push. She pushes the window open; wind lifts her hair; rain blows in. Round sunglasses from her jacket. On. At night. The skyline reflected across both lenses. She tilts her head, checking the fit. A tiny grin. She thinks they look good.

### SHOT 29. THE WINDOW LEDGE
Interior wide toward the window (24mm), slow dolly. She climbs onto the sill; her silhouette against the city; the room almost dark. She crouches, testing her balance; the backpack shifts; she adjusts it. The camera inches closer until the window frame surrounds her like a portal. A drone crosses far below; its red light on the wet glass. She looks down.

### SHOT 30. FIVE STOREYS ABOVE KILN STREET
Her POV, extreme high angle (18mm), slow tilt down: wet pavement, headlights, a pedestrian under an umbrella, a delivery bike between cars; the full drop; a car sends water at the curb; traffic rises. Profile on the sill: she releases one hand, then the other, balanced on her legs. Close-up: her smile.
**ROBIN** Let's go.

### SHOT 31. THE JUMP
**1. Exterior side profile**, slightly below the window: she pushes off; hair and jacket trailing; for two or three frames horizontal against the city.
**2. Rear tracking**: the facade rushing up; the street expanding; her jacket's lines activate; the soles flicker blue; wind.
**3. ECU of the shoe**, the camera rotating beneath her feet: the coils compress, glow; a sharp mechanical THUMP. Not a stop, not flight: a violent redirection of momentum. The blue flashes again.
**4. Wide city**: she arcs away from the building, under a fire escape, over a parked car; the camera rotates with her until the city is briefly sideways. For one instant she seems to be flying. Then gravity.
**5. Player perspective**: directly behind her, just above her shoulder; the camera steadies; rooftops, ledges, fire escapes ahead. Only wind and the shoes' low pulse. Still falling. A rooftop edge rushes toward the centre; the camera centres the nearest landing surface.
**GAMEPLAY: THE SHOES.** The player takes control mid-fall. No button prompt. No tutorial overlay. No voice-over. CUT TO BLACK. **END OF COLD OPEN.**

# DIRECTING NOTES
1. A distinct camera language for each sequence: the WFC prologue precise, controlled, surveillance-like, symmetrical, cold; the bedroom loose, reactive, layered, warm practical lights, music-driven cuts; the jump fast and kinetic, gradually aligning with the player's view.
2. Foreground objects make the flat feel inhabited: a hanging jacket, a screwdriver, a desk lamp, a guitar neck, a doorframe between the camera and her.
3. Don't cut to every object. Let the camera discover details; dedicated inserts only where an object changes the story.
4. Let it breathe: the closed door, the note, the silence after the song. Stillness makes the action land.
5. Design the ending around the handoff: the last camera move aligns the camera, her trajectory and the next landing; the first playable frame keeps her speed, orientation and position. The player inherits her momentum.
